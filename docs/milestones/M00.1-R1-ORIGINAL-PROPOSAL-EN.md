## Scope and historical problem

M00.1 evaluates checkout, accepted pricing snapshot, inventory reservation, PaymentAttempt, Stripe Checkout Session, financial evidence, reconciliation, post-capture commercial obligation, refunds, compensations and recoverable failures. M00.2 owns individual DigitalLicense assignment and inventory physical modeling. M00.3 owns detailed guest claim, identity and authorization. M00.4 owns cross-context contract-map consolidation.

The core timing conflict: a historical fifteen-minute reservation cannot be released merely by clock time once Stripe Checkout has been enabled, because Stripe Session expiration can exceed that interval and a complete Session can still have unsettled money.

R1 proposed a five-state reservation timeline: INITIAL_HELD, PAYMENT_HELD, SETTLEMENT_HELD, RELEASE_REVIEW, COMMITTED, RELEASED. These are six named values when terminal values are counted; some R1 narratives described five phases plus terminal outcomes. The subsequent accepted D-01 policy retains protection while allowing AUD-P1-01 to select a *candidate* simpler four-state Reservation representation: RESERVED, PAYMENT_PROTECTED, COMMITTED, RELEASED. Neither physical FSM is finally ratified. Do not silently migrate R1 settlement/review conditions into terminal release: their semantics remain represented by Payments and the durable saga.

## Original aggregate and context candidates

- Cart: authoritative persisted guest/authenticated purchase intent, not an Order or financial truth.
- Quote/Pricing: exact money rules, accepted re-pricing and invariant snapshot.
- Ordering/Order: immutable accepted order identity and commercial obligation.
- Ordering/CheckoutProcess: durable step coordination, deadline and pending commands, not an independent finance owner.
- Inventory/StockPool and Reservation: capacity, protected hold, commit and release.
- Payments/Payment and PaymentAttempt: provider-neutral financial identity, attempts, Session/PaymentIntent references and pending results.
- Payments/Refund: independently tracked provider refund with its actual outcome.
- Fulfillment/DigitalLicense: unique license assignment, secure disclosure and audit.
- WebhookInbox/Outbound work: recoverable events and business effects, not transport ACK-based commercial truth.

R1 suggested transition guards and an eventual multi-context saga; later ratifications tightened it by requiring pre-HTTP durable operation identity, strict local atomicity, exclusive admission, persistent closure barriers, unknown-result handling and neutralization of previously enabled dispatchers.

## Original checkout transition sequence

1. Authenticate or authorize the actor and store/tenant context; verify cart contents and accepted price snapshot.
2. Persist Order/CheckoutProcess identity before any financial exposure; preserve immutable economic terms.
3. Atomically reserve inventory within Inventory, including the final unit and multi-line constraints.
4. Upgrade protection durably before Payments may enable a potentially collectible Stripe operation.
5. Payments admits a stable PaymentAttempt, persists external operation parameters and idempotency scope.
6. Stripe Checkout creation occurs outside MongoDB transactions. A missing reply is UNKNOWN, not FAILED.
7. Stripe/webhook reconciliation establishes the financial outcome; verified Inbox and mandatory Outbox handoff prevent lost obligations.
8. Ordering incorporates the financial fact and continues the saga, Inventory commits capacity; Fulfillment handles unique key access.
9. If fulfillment fails, reconcile original inventory work, attempt safe recovery, and use an exclusive documented refund branch only when valid.
10. Cancel/expire cases reconcile all prior external exposures before a versioned Inventory release.
